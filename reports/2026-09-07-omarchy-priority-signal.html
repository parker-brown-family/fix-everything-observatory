Ranking the omarchy queue

Priority is not readable from GitHub's own fields in this repo. There is no severity vocabulary to read, every engagement signal is saturated at zero, and the only queue that exists is one maintainer's attention — currently being outrun seventeen to one. Ranking it is off the table: priority in this project is founder vision, not a number an instrument can compute. Collapsing it is not. 939 of 2,000 sampled open items are duplicates of 371 distinct surfaces, and that map is now built.

Read first

  1. 1

    The queue is a person, so a ranking ranks his next hour

    Of 232 merges sampled since Aug 1, dhh performed 186; ryanrhughes 36, spencerbull 8. Three humans, and one of them does eighty percent.

    measured Nothing to accept or reject here — it constrains every design below. "High priority" can only mean worth the next hour of a maintainer's attention, never "important in the abstract".

  2. 2

    Every engagement field is saturated at zero

    In a 500-issue sample of the open backlog, 475 had zero reactions and none exceeded two. 1,030 of 1,490 open issues carry no label at all. 667 have no comment.

    measured Delete engagement from any scoring plan. A field with no dynamic range cannot rank, and a swarm can manufacture the range trivially if you start rewarding it.

  3. 3

    The missing thing is the review, not the information

    A review is the single strongest merge predictor measured — 82% merge with one, 57% without — and 1,966 of 1,967 open PRs have none. Meanwhile 630 open issues already have a linked PR.

    measured There are two queues, not one. The review-bound queue has a fix already written and a human-shaped bottleneck, and it is where the collapse pays first: every duplicate patch that merges into one is a review a human never has to do.

  4. 4

    Age measures discovery here, not difficulty

    Median time to merge is 2.3 hours; to close unmerged, 0.7. Decisions are near-instant once the item is seen.

    inferred An item still open after a few days was almost certainly never read. That reframes the product question from ranking to routing — and it makes recency a strong baseline any model has to beat.

  5. 5

    Recurrence is the only severity signal with any range

    51 open issues carry "lock screen" in the title. Bigram clustering over 1,000 open titles: omarchy update 21, omarchy install 18, bar widget 15, quickshell sigsegv 13, hot reload 11.

    measured Clustered for real since this brief was first written: 939 of the 2,000 sampled items are duplicates of 371 surfaces, and the contamination test came back 20 of 20 — the same top surfaces with or without agent-smelling reporters. Recurrence is not a ranking signal. It is a merge key.

the number that decides the design

1,966 of 1,967 open PRs have no review. The one signal that predicts a merge is absent from essentially the entire open queue — so the ranking problem is not "which of these is good", it is "which one should a human look at first". Those counts were taken minutes apart while the queue was moving, hence one more than the total.

Findings

The repo has no priority vocabulary, and adding one would not help

measured The label set is GitHub's defaults plus device specific, intel macs and claude-code-assisted. No severity, no priority, no milestones in use. Labelling tracks attention rather than leading it: 16% of the last week's arrivals are labelled, against 78% of the issues that have survived a month.

Invalidation: if the share of labelled issues among new arrivals rises above roughly half in any week, triage has started running ahead of the queue and labels become a legitimate input. Re-run the two counts before believing this finding again.

Engagement metrics would actively invert the queue

measured This is stronger than "reactions are sparse". On the 420 closed issues sampled, every engagement signal correlated with a longer life: reactions ≥3 took 65× longer to close than the rest, comments ≥5 73× longer, four or more participants 100× longer. Attention accumulates on the ones nobody can fix.

Invalidation: re-run the same buckets on issues closed after Sep 1 only. If the flood-era items reverse the sign, this was a property of the pre-flood repo and the finding dies.

There are two queues and they need different instruments

measured 630 open issues have a linked PR and 633 open PRs close an issue: a fix is written and waiting on a human. The other ~860 have no proposed fix at all. Ranking these against each other is a category error — the first asks which patch should be reviewed next, the second asks which bug matters.

Invalidation: if the linked-PR issues merge at a similar rate to unlinked ones closing, the partition buys nothing and one queue is fine.

The swarm is the largest single voice in the data it would be ranked by

inferred Recurrence is the best severity proxy available and also the easiest for an agent fleet to inflate, since a fleet can file the same finding from many accounts faster than humans can hit the bug. The observatory already scores agent-smell per item; recurrence has to be counted over distinct human reporters or the instrument ends up measuring its own reflection.

Invalidation: compute recurrence twice, with and without agent-smelling reporters. If the top ten surfaces are the same list either way, the contamination is not material and the dedupe can be dropped.

The collapse map

Built after the decision below, on the same 2,000 sampled open items. Four kinds of edge, kept separate so a cluster is defended by the evidence that formed it rather than by one opaque score: a PR that closes an issue, a #mention pointing at another open item, two PRs touching the same rare file, and text agreement above a cosine of 0.55 on the title and first 1,500 characters of the body. Clusters are connected components; every edge that is not ground truth must also clear a topical floor before it can merge anything.

the invalidation test, run

The brief promised that recurrence had to survive its own falsification: compute the top twenty surfaces with and without agent-smelling reporters, and if the list is the same either way, agent inflation is not material. The overlap is 20 of 20. Only 102 of the 939 clustered items smell agentic at all, and 252 of the 371 clusters carry two or more distinct low-smell reporters. Recurrence stands, and the identity-dedupe is a guard rather than a correction — worth keeping cheap, not worth building a subsystem for.

What replaces the ranking

The three-axis priority model this brief originally proposed is dead — see the decision below. What survives is one of its inputs, repurposed. Recurrence was proposed as a way to rank; it is much better at merging, because a collapse needs no judgement about what matters, only evidence that two tickets are one thing.

Was going to beIs nowWhy
Blast radius, scored per item Dropped Severity is the founder's call. An instrument that scores it is guessing at vision and will be wrong in a way nobody can argue with.
Independent recurrence, as a rank input The collapse key Same computation, no judgement required. Two tickets are one thing or they are not, and the evidence is inspectable.
Doneness, as a rank input Cluster metadata Which member of a cluster carries the strongest patch is exactly what a collapse needs to decide what survives.
Unranked as a first-class state Kept, unchanged An item the clusterer never read is uncollapsed, not unique. The hollow outline still means never scored.
the gate, revised

The two-week prediction test was written for a ranking, and there is no longer a ranking to test. The equivalent gate for a collapse is precision on the merge proposal: of the clusters proposed, how many would a maintainer accept as one surface? That is checkable today by hand on a sample rather than in a fortnight, and it is a stricter test — a wrong collapse closes somebody's ticket, while a wrong rank only wastes a scroll.

Decided

Answered in the margins of this page on Sep 8, and recorded here so the document carries the decision rather than the question. Nothing on this page is waiting on you.

QuestionAnswerWhat it changed
Does the observatory take a priority axis? No. "Priority is too hard and founder vision based." The three-axis model is deleted, not deferred. The instrument stays an attribution instrument, and this brief became a collapse plan instead.
Which queue first? Review-bound. Concurred. Still right, and the collapse serves it directly: the review queue shrinks by every duplicate PR that collapses into one.
Scoring budget per item? Not the constraint. Keep good records, make multiple passes that each work differently from the last. The map is built from four independent edge kinds rather than one score, and every edge is recorded with its kind so a later pass can disagree with an earlier one.
Does a gate stand between the work and the UI? Yes. Agreed. Reworked into a collapse-precision check, since there is no ranking left to predict against.
what is not settled, and what triggers it

Nothing goes near omacom/omarchy until you say so. The plan file holds a draft comment per cluster, written and unposted. Posting 371 cross-reference comments into someone else's repository is a public act on a project we do not own, and the flood is already the thing making that repository hard to work in — arriving with an automated de-duplication campaign is the kind of help that reads as more noise. The trigger for that decision is a maintainer saying they want it, or a hand-verified sample proving the precision, whichever comes first.

Method and queries

omacom/omarchy · 2026-09-07

What was sampled

  • 420 closed issues since Jul 1, whole population for that window, via GraphQL search with stateReason, reactions, comments, participants, labels and cross-reference counts.
  • 300 closed PRs of 354 since Aug 1, with additions, deletions, changed files, review count, merged-by and author association.
  • 1,000 open issues and 1,000 open PRs, newest first. GitHub's search API refuses to page past 1,000 results, which is why the age histograms show no bucket beyond thirty days: that tail was never fetched. It is unfetched, not empty — the direct count says 209 open issues predate Aug 8 and 173 predate Jun 9.
  • Whole-population total_count queries wherever a count sufficed, so the headline numbers are not sample estimates.

The queries that matter

Confounds hit along the way

  • The first PR pass asked GraphQL for participants alongside additions and deletions across 500 nodes and was refused with Resource limits for this query exceeded — it returned nulls rather than an error, which would have read as "no PRs closed". Re-run at 50 nodes a page without participants.
  • The REST search endpoint rate-limits at 30 calls a minute; a first snapshot run returned null for every count. Those nulls looked exactly like zeros. Re-run with a 2.5-second spacing and a retry.
  • Review counts total 1,968 against 1,967 open PRs because the queries ran minutes apart while PRs were still arriving.

Two figures that disagreed, and which one is right

A 500-issue sample put the labelled share of open issues at 15.8%; the whole-population count puts it at 30.9% (460 of 1,490). Both are correct — the sample was drawn newest-first, and new arrivals are much less likely to be labelled than survivors. That gap is the finding, not an error: labelling lags arrival badly enough to be visible as a sampling artifact.

That is what makes recurrence useful — it names the subsystem carrying the most distinct failures, which is a better unit of work than any single ticket.

Why it needs identity-dedupe

The top open-issue authors in the sample file between four and twelve issues each, and the top open-PR author has 74 open. A recurrence count that does not divide by reporter will rank whatever the busiest fleet is currently interested in, which is a measurement of the fleet rather than of the operating system.

What is not established

No test has been run linking recurrence to actual maintainer action. It is the most promising candidate axis and it is still a candidate. The two-week freeze-and-check is what would move it to measured.

How the collapse map was built

2,000 sampled open items · four edge kinds

Edges, and what each is worth

  • closes — a PR's closingIssuesReferences. Ground truth; always merges.
  • mentions — a #1234 in the first 2,500 characters of a body, pointing at another item in the sample. Cheap and plentiful, and the most dangerous: a passing reference is not a relationship.
  • files — two PRs touching the same file, where that file is touched by no more than five PRs in the sample, and the pair shares at least two such files.
  • text — cosine over tf-idf of the title (weighted ×3) plus the first 1,500 characters of the body, threshold 0.55. Terms appearing in more than 20% of documents are dropped, which is what stops the word "omarchy" from clustering the entire repository.

Ground-truth edges merge unconditionally. Everything else must also clear a topical floor of 0.22 cosine before it is allowed to merge anything, and any node with more than six merging edges is treated as a bridge: it joins its single strongest neighbour's cluster rather than fusing every cluster it touches.

The bug that came first, and why it is in this brief

The first run produced a cluster of 1,025 items — half the sample in one blob. Shared files at a loose rarity threshold chained the graph transitively: A and B touch one file, B and C touch another, and by the tenth hop a keyboard-backlight bug is in the same cluster as a locale bug in the clock. The number looked spectacular and meant nothing.

Three changes fixed it: the file rarity threshold dropped from twelve PRs to five and now requires two shared files, non-ground-truth edges gained the topical floor, and bridges stopped carrying unions. The largest cluster went from 1,025 to 16, and total coverage fell from 71% to 47% — a smaller claim, and the first one that survives being looked at.

Worth stating plainly: coverage is a tuning artifact. A looser threshold "collapses" more of the queue and means less. The 47% is the number that held up when the clusters were read.

What this cannot see

  • The sample is the newest 1,000 issues and 1,000 PRs, GitHub's search ceiling — 1,000 of 1,490 open issues and 1,000 of 1,967 open PRs. Older duplicates exist and are not in it.
  • Clusters were verified by reading the top twenty, not all 371. The tail is unaudited.
  • No cluster has been checked against a maintainer's own judgement, which is the only verdict that decides whether a collapse is correct.

What this brief does not cover

limits

Method blind spots

  • Everything here is API metadata. Nothing read an issue body, and blast radius — the axis recommended as primary — is precisely the one that requires reading them. That axis is therefore proposed, never measured.
  • The closed-issue signal table is mostly pre-flood. 420 issues closed since Jul 1, when the repo was taking 24 issues a week. Whether those correlations survive at 528 a week is untested, and it is the first thing that would change the recommendation.
  • No timeline reconstruction. Who closed each issue, and whether a maintainer had commented before it closed, was not fetched — that would separate "dhh saw it" from "it aged out", which is the mechanism this whole brief infers rather than observes.
  • Merge-rate comparisons are not causal. PRs with a review merge more often; a review also happens because a maintainer already thought the PR was plausible. The arrow could run either way, and for the purpose here — where to send attention — it does not matter, but it would matter for a claim like "asking for reviews raises merge rates".

What would strengthen it cheaply

  • Pull the closing actor and pre-close maintainer comments for 200 recent closures. One query, and it converts the central inference into a measurement.
  • Re-run every bucket on Sep-only closures to test the flood-era reversal.
  • Score 50 issues by hand for blast radius and check inter-rater agreement with a model before building anything that scores 1,490.

Standing interest

This brief argues for a feature in an instrument we own, on data that instrument already mines. The cheap axis is the one already in the pipeline and the expensive axis is the one recommended as primary, which is at least the uncomfortable direction for a conflict of interest to run.
