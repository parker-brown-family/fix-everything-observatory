Read these first

  1. The headline counts three different things as one, and one of them is not duplication

    219 of the 756 are tickets that close when their own fix merges. Calling that a duplicate makes a fix landing look like waste. Splitting the number three ways costs one line and changes what a maintainer does with it.

  2. 43 “duplicate patches” are one person deliberately splitting a job

    Same author on both sides. Of the four such pairs read, none was a duplicate: sections 1–2 and section 3 of one issue, the debug warning and the sanitiser, the hotkey filter and the headset filter. Nobody duplicates their own patch by accident.

What the reading found

Same surface is not the same work, and that is the whole gap

measuredRoughly nine in ten of the pairs read do share a surface: the same file, the same function, the same service, the same piece of hardware. Grouping them is useful and the worklist is right to. But of those, only about half are redundant — the other half are two different faults in one place, or one job split across two patches, or a narrow fix sitting inside a broad one.

Two patches on the network panel, sharing a file and two added lines: one widens the IPv4 rows, the other adds IPv6 rows, and the first says in its own body that it is doing this independently of the second. Both should merge. Neither is a duplicate of the other.

A shared rare literal proves a shared subsystem, not a shared defect

measuredThis was the single largest source of “strong” evidence in the re-score — 500 of the 598 strong links. It is also the weakest of them. Two Bluetooth reports both quoting bt-agent.service and bluez-tools scored as strong agreement; one is a pairing agent with no terminal to prompt on, the other is a unit-ordering race that stops the agent starting at all. Author identity is the cheapest correction available

inferredSame author on both sides of a patch pair meant a deliberate split every time it was read (0 of 4 were duplicates). One field, already fetched, splitting 70 rows the right way.

Inferred rather than measured: four and two reads are not a rate. The rule is cheap to test and cheap to reverse.

The queue moves faster than any report about it

measured113 of the 1,072 grouped items were already settled 15 hours after the worklist was generated — 72 patches merged, 41 items closed. Any worklist over this repository is perishable, and a section of it is wrong before it is read.

The one link that never missed is the one the tool half-reads

measuredGitHub's own closing reference held 42 times out of 42. Yours to decide

Does the worklist get regenerated with the split, or does the correction ride alongside it?

The tool computes one number by subtracting group count from member count. Splitting it three ways is a small change to plan() and changes every headline it has printed.

Recommendation: regenerate. The current headline is the first thing a maintainer reads, and it is the part that does not survive.

Do same-author patch pairs become their own bucket rather than a duplicate claim?

43 rows. “One job, split across two patches” is still worth showing a maintainer — they conflict with each other — but it is a different sentence and a different action.

Recommendation: yes, as a separate bucket. It is one comparison on data already fetched, and it removes the most confidently wrong rows in the file.

Does a disclaimer in the body veto a group, or only demote it?

32 rows carry text naming the partner and denying it. Of the four read, one was a duplicate anyway — a narrow fix inside a broad one, where the disclaimer was about scope, not identity.

Recommendation: demote, do not veto. Show the sentence on the row and let the reader spend the five seconds.

Does any of this go upstream?

Nothing has been posted. The corrected worklist is more defensible than the original, but it still proposes closing other people's tickets on a 43–50% base rate.

Recommendation: not as a collapse proposal. The near-certain tier alone — identical re-filings, patches declaring the same target, threads where a collaborator already said duplicate — would be defensible, and it is a much smaller list.

What would overturn this

invalidation

Read 30 more pairs per class and the rates move outside the intervals printed here. Or: apply the same-author rule and find, in a larger sample, that same-author patch pairs are duplicates as often as any other pair — four reads is not a rate, and that rule carries 43 rows on its own. Or: the adjudications above are one reader's calls on a strict standard, that a row is redundant only if closing it loses nothing. Loosen the standard to “a maintainer would want these on one screen” and the number goes back up toward 700.

How the evidence was scored

omacom/omarchy · 2026-09-08

Fetch

Every item in a group was re-read from GitHub's GraphQL API: state, body, labels, up to 30 comments, and the cross-reference and duplicate-mark events on its timeline. Every grouped pull request had its full file list and patch text pulled from the REST endpoint — 625 requests, 9.3 MB of diff.

Pair evidence

  • same target — two patches whose closingIssuesReferences intersect.
  • same deleted line — both patches remove the same normalised source line from the same file, where fewer than 12 grouped patches remove it. Deleting a line is stronger than adding one: two people can add different things to one place, but removing the same line means both decided the same code was wrong.
  • same added line — the same, for additions.
  • said duplicate — a comment matching duplicate-of / same-as / already-reported-in naming the partner, or a MarkedAsDuplicate event.
  • rare literals — backticked spans, paths, quoted strings and identifiers shared by both bodies and appearing in at most four of the 1,072. Two or more make a strong link, one makes a supporting one.

Standard applied when reading

A row counts as redundant only if closing it loses nothing — no case, no detail, no patch a maintainer would otherwise take. Two reports of one bug: redundant. Two bugs in one service: not. A narrow patch entirely contained in a broad one: redundant. Two halves of one job: not.

That is a strict standard, and it is the one the word “duplicate” implies in a line that proposes closing things. A looser standard — “worth reading together” — would put the number back near 700, and the worklist would be right to group them either way.

What the intervals are

Wilson score intervals at 95%, on 30 / 30 / 42 / 18 reads. Not a bootstrap over the whole file: the sample is the measurement, and the population count is exact.
